# Reference Office V1

Generated with OpenAI image generation from the user-provided office design.
The room, blank wall whiteboard, conference furniture and desk accessories are
separate layers. Characters and workstation animations use the existing packs.
Board content is rendered dynamically from fixed structured task data;
there are no baked text, diagrams or charts in the frame image.

- `conference-table.png`: meeting table and chairs; runtime footprint is 8 x 3 cells.
- `desk-accessories.png`: plants, mug and books; cropped into separate sprites
  whose visible bases attach to the workstation's measured tabletop surface.
- `room-whiteboard-v4.png`: active boxed-office background, 1424 x 1105, with
  both front wall-top corners aligned near image y=743. V5 was rejected because
  its left front wall corner was about 128 pixels too high.
  The full perimeter walls, bookcase, wall-mounted board, pantry and right-side
  windows remain visible. The image is fitted with its original aspect ratio,
  without enlargement or cropping. The board frame and shadow stay in the image;
  only live content is overlaid. Ordinary and civic offices share this background.
- `room-workplace-v1.png`: active collaboration background, 1774 x 887, with
  a pure-white floor and continuous flat rear wall, filing cabinets, binders,
  archive boxes, a water dispenser, a noticeboard, a calendar and clock.
  Window-side storage holds a coffee machine, kettle, cups and document trays.
  The copier, file trolley, shredder and raised wall strip beside the windows
  have been removed. No lounge chair, rug or cushions remain.
  Nine-slice rendering preserves the side furniture and
  upper-wall proportions; only the quiet central wall and lower floor extend
  to fill the viewport. The window keeps 400
  presentation units of width while the unchanged 1100 x 950 interaction area
  is centered horizontally in the entire scene.
- `whiteboard-adaptive-v1.png`: separate collaboration board, 2097 x 750.
  The live 920 x 314 content surface uses 28 px type and a uniform 0.9 scale.
  The 900 x 302 frame is about 58% larger in area than its first adaptive version.
  The frame and content stay centered above the workstation area; both follow
  the center of the entire viewport. Actor, desk and navigation coordinates
  are unchanged. See `room-workplace-v1-prompts.md` for the current background
  generation and registration; `whiteboard-adaptive-v1-prompts.md` records the board asset.

WebP alternatives are built by `scripts/build-office-assets.mjs`.

Assembly: `referenceOfficeScenePack` and `createReferenceOfficePresentation()`
are exported by `@pixoffice/scene-office`. The complete office example selects
this layout; the original `officeScenePack` remains available to consumers.
The logical map is 22 x 17 integer cells, with a 1100 x 850 presentation plane.
Ordinary and civic workstations and character height share a 1.5x scale around
the seat anchor. Collaboration uses 1.875x, enlarging the team and workstations
by 25% while keeping the room, whiteboard and navigation geometry unchanged.
Monitor anchors, tabletop accessories and work clouds follow the same ratio;
seated collaboration nameplates sit beside the desks to avoid the preceding
row's chair backs. Standing and walking names remain above the characters.
Custom current-format saved maps keep their geometry;
retired save formats are not migrated.
Names follow characters. Task cards attach to the right-edge midpoint of their home
monitor using the same drawing geometry as the computer sprite. They remain at
the workstation when a character leaves. Titles, subtitles and known percentages
come from task data; received progress changes ease over 280 ms without changing
business state. The card's left-center pointer targets the monitor.
Only the card surface uses 50% opacity; text and progress remain opaque. A card
folds into one task-title row when a character enters its full visual area, then
restores after 180 ms clear. Hovering or selecting intentionally opens details.
Occlusion tests retain the full card area while folded to avoid flicker.

The wall board defaults to a fixed two-column `state.taskBoard` presentation:
status and task summary on the left, up to three numbered progress rows on the
right, plus a note and an optional HTTP(S) project link. The office example
derives the content from its current team's task data. Missing progress is
shown as unknown, not zero. This is scene-local presentation data, not a new
transport protocol. Lucide icons are generated by
`scripts/assets/build-whiteboard-icons.mjs` under their ISC license, included in
`public/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`.

The ordinary office board uses the same 778 x 314 desktop content surface as the
expanded reader, with a 26 px preview type scale. Only the complete
surface is scaled to the board face; typography and columns are never squeezed
independently. The preview surface is transparent: only live content is drawn
inside the background's existing board. No extra physical frame, wall patch or
opaque content panel is added. The presentation transform does not alter the
prop's business position, task state or meeting anchors.
In-scene progress rows also use a transparent, shadow-free surface so the
whiteboard material remains visible. The expanded reader retains its paper
surface; both use the same layout and task data.
The active wall-height edit and measured calibration are in `whiteboard-v4-prompts.md`.
Discarded background variants and their generated copies have been removed.

The wall board accepts only the fixed `state.taskBoard` structure. It has no
Markdown, HTML or legacy text fallback.
